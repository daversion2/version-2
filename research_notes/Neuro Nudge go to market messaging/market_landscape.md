So **every figure below comes from search-result snippets and summaries, not from reading the full source page.** The URLs point to the page the snippet came from. ## 1. Market size and growth by category

### Takeaway
App-store consumer spending on Health & Fitness reached a record **~$4.5B in 2025, up about 13% YoY, on flat downloads (~3.96B, +0.8%)** (Sensor Tower). Money is growing because each user pays more, not because more people are downloading. The US accounts for more than half of category spend. Meditation is the one sub-category clearly shrinking at the leader level. "Habit tracker" market-size figures are unreliable, and bottom-up estimates put it at about $100M.

### Cited Findings
**Health & Fitness overall (app-store spend, the strongest data)**
- Sensor Tower: Health & Fitness apps hit a record **$4.5B in in-app purchase revenue in 2025**; downloads rose **0.8% to 3.96B**. Several outlets report revenue as **+13% YoY**. This counts store purchases only; web checkout and B2B/employer revenue come on top — [Business of Apps news](https://www.businessofapps.com/news/apps-capture-larger-share-health-fitness-market-2025/); [Sahha summary](https://sahha.ai/blog/health-fitness-app-market-2026/)
- The **US took more than half of global Health & Fitness consumer spending in 2024** (Sensor Tower data, cited secondhand) — [Business of Apps H&F report](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-report/)
- Business of Apps (broader definition): Health & Fitness apps made **close to $6B in 2025, +17%**. About **80% of H&F app revenue comes from subscriptions**, the rest from ads — [Business of Apps H&F report](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-report/)
- Business of Apps split: **fitness apps $3.4B in 2025 (+24.5%)**; **health apps $3.5B in 2025 (+23.5%)** — [BoA fitness](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/fitness-app-market/); [BoA health](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/health-app-market/)
- Statista projects **US fitness apps at US$2.49B in 2025** (paid apps plus IAP only) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/outlook/hmo/digital-health/digital-fitness-well-being/health-wellness-coaching/fitness-apps/united-states)

**Fitness apps (analyst "market size", a looser definition)**
- Grand View Research: global fitness apps market **USD 12.1B in 2025**, forecast **$13.9B in 2026 and $33.6B by 2033 (13.4% CAGR, 2026–33)**. An older Grand View edition said $23.21B by 2030 at 13.88% CAGR — [Grand View press release](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/press-release/global-fitness-app-market); [Grand View report page](https://grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/fitness-app-market)
- Emergen Research: **USD 9.82B in 2025** — [Emergen](https://www.emergenresearch.com/industry-report/fitness-app-market)

**Meditation / mindfulness**

- Straits Research gives a similar ~$2.2–2.25B for 2025 but an **18.5% CAGR** — [Straits](https://straitsresearch.com/report/meditation-management-apps-market)
- The category leaders are shrinking: Calm's estimated revenue **fell 24% in 2025** and Headspace's subscriber base fell too (see section 2). This contradicts the double-digit CAGR forecasts above.

**Wellness apps (umbrella)**

**Habit trackers (low-quality data, wide disagreement)**

- Dataintelo's own bottom-up company triangulation puts the market at **about USD 100M for 2025**, far below its headline $1.7B — [Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/habit-tracking-apps-market)

**Nutrition / diet**
- One market report (openPR press release, low quality) forecasts diet and nutrition apps reaching **USD 18.2B by 2036 at 12.1% CAGR** — [openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4600577/diet-nutrition-apps-market-to-expand-at-12-1-cagr-through-2036)
- I found no Sensor Tower or Appfigures nutrition-only spend figure.

**Social fitness**
- Verified Market Reports (cited secondhand): social fitness apps **$3.5B in 2024 to $10.8B by 2033 (13.5% CAGR)**. Unverified — [Trygoals blog citing VMR](https://www.trygoals.co/blog/best-fitness-accountability-apps-2026)

### Inferences
- For a go-to-market deck, **Sensor Tower's $4.5B (2025, store-only, +13%)** is the most defensible headline. Add "plus web and B2B" rather than quoting the $12B or $33B analyst figures.
- Flat downloads with rising revenue mean the category is mature and fought over: growth comes from raising prices and converting more users to paid, not from finding new users. A new entrant has to win attention from incumbents rather than ride category growth.
- The meditation CAGR forecasts are at odds with what the category leaders actually earn. Be cautious about pitching Neuro-Nudge's Meditate as a "growing market" play.
- Habit tracking is a small, fragmented revenue pool (likely around $100M rather than billions). Positioning against "habit trackers" means positioning in a small space.

## 2. Leading players: revenue, downloads, pricing

### Takeaway
The market splits into **(a) hardware-plus-subscription platforms worth ~$10B each (Oura, Whoop)**, **(b) one large social platform (Strava, ~$415–490M ARR, IPO filed) that is buying up niche apps**, **(c) mid-size subscription leaders (Calm ~$210M, Headspace ~$140M, both shrinking; Noom; MyFitnessPal ~$250–310M)** and **(d) a long tail of focused indies** making $0.3M–30M a year (Hevy, Strong, Fitbod, Opal, Finch, Streaks, One Sec). The indies that do well own one job: lifting logs, blocking apps, or a self-care pet.

### Inferences
- **Price anchors for Neuro-Nudge's buyer**: logging-only lifting apps sit at $24–96/yr (Hevy at ~$36/yr is the low anchor). Ecosystem memberships cost $70–100/yr (Oura $69.99, Strava $79.99, Google $99, Opal $99.99). Bundles reach $150/yr (Strava+Runna). A bundle of five micro-apps can credibly price in the **$60–100/yr** band if it is framed as one membership rather than another logger.
- **Consolidation is real.** Strava bought Runna and The Breakaway, MyFitnessPal bought Cal AI, Sword bought Headspace (and Kaia), and Ladder added nutrition. The "all-in-one" direction is validated, but incumbents are getting there by acquisition.
- **Single-founder-scale wins are proven.** Opal ($10M ARR, 11 people) and Finch (~$30M) show that a sharply framed behavioural app can reach eight figures without a wearable.

## 3. Pricing, monetization patterns and retention benchmarks

### Takeaway
Health & Fitness converts trials better than any other category (median **~38% trial-to-paid**, top quartile ~51%) and keeps annual subscribers well. But only **~3–8% of installs are still active at day 30**, and only **~2.9% of downloads pay within 35 days**. Hard paywalls convert about **5x better** than freemium. **5–9 day trials beat longer ones** in this category.

### Cited Findings
**RevenueCat, State of Subscription Apps 2026** (read via search snippets; report is gated)
- Health & Fitness **median trial-to-paid 37.7%, top quartile >51.4%**, down from **39.9% in the 2025 report** — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-monetization-first-month/)
- Health & Fitness **median download-to-trial 6.9%**; **median download-to-paid within 35 days 2.9%** (top quartile >6.2%) — [Sahha summarizing RevenueCat](https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-monetization-first-month/)
- Trial length in Health & Fitness: **5–9 day trials convert at 46.8% vs 40.4% for 10–16 days**. Across all categories, trials of ≤4 days convert at 25.5% and 17–32 days at 42.5% — [RevenueCat trial-length blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)
- Yearly retention: **Health & Fitness 86.4%, Productivity 85.6%, the top two categories**. The metric's definition was not visible in the snippet; it may not mean subscriber retention — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- **Hard paywall vs freemium**: median day-35 conversion **10.7% vs 2.1%** (hard paywall down from 12.1% in 2025). Median revenue per install at day 14: **$2.32 vs $0.27** (third-party summary) — [RevenueCat 2026 trends blog](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [Medium summary](https://chuvak-pavel.medium.com/state-of-app-monetization-2026-key-trends-from-revenuecat-and-adapty-8bdc23a4bb6f)
- **AI apps**: a third-party reading of SOSA 2026 says AI apps make **41% more revenue per user** but **churn annual subscribers about 30% faster** (79% of annual subscribers lost before month 13) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a)
- The **2025 report (2024 data)** put Health & Fitness medians at **$39.99/yr and $9.70/mo**, Y1 LTV **$27.21**, and revenue per install at day 60 of **$0.63** (double the $0.31 all-category median) — [Airbridge citing SOSA 2025](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark); [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)

**Adapty, State of In-App Subscriptions 2026** (a separate dataset)
- Health & Fitness trial-to-paid **35.0%**, the highest of any category (all-category average 25.6%). But **first-renewal retention is 30.3%, the lowest of any category** — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)

**Engagement retention (installs still active)**
- Health & Fitness **day-30 ≈3%** (Business of Apps, 2023 data, older) — [fitnessrefined](https://fitnessrefined.co/fitness-app-retention-statistics/); [The Run Rate](https://therunrate.co/studio-growth/fitness-app-retention-3-percent-day-30-activation/)
- AppsFlyer-based: Fitness **day-30 ≈3.7%**; **day-1 ≈19–21%** — [Spreadcast](https://spreadcast.app/resources/app-retention-benchmarks); [GetStream](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/)
- UXCam: Health & Fitness **day-1 ≈25%, day-30 8–12%**; all-category medians **day-1 ≈25%, day-30 ≈4%** — [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/)

**Observed paywall patterns**
- Opal: freemium with low conversion (9%) but a large base (1M DAU) and a high price ($99.99/yr) — [note.com](https://note.com/kmna/n/n9e147e37f154?hl=en)
- One Sec: low price ($19.99/yr) with a 1-week trial — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/one-sec-screen-time-focus/)
- Hevy: generous free tier plus a $74.99 lifetime option — [SensAI](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/hevy-review-2026)
- Wearable AI coaches are being folded into existing memberships: Whoop Coach comes included; Oura Advisor comes with membership; Google charges $9.99/mo for Premium — [yukaichou](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/top-10-gamification-in-fitness/); [helsa.health](https://helsa.health/compare/best-ai-health-coach-apps)

### Inferences
- Benchmarks to plan against: about **7% download-to-trial, about 38% trial-to-paid, therefore about 2.9% download-to-paid**. At a ~$40–60 annual price, that is roughly **$1.20–1.75 of first-year revenue per install** before refunds and store fees. This caps paid-acquisition cost per install near **$1**.
- A **7-day trial** is the best-evidenced choice for this category. A hard paywall after onboarding converts about 5x better than freemium, but freemium (the Opal model) can work if the free tier creates word of mouth. Pods could be that word-of-mouth engine.
- Adapty's lowest-of-any-category first-renewal rate (30%) means H&F apps are good at the first sale and bad at the second. **Retention past the first renewal is where the money is lost.** An app whose core metric shows the user that they are getting better at starting may address exactly that.
- The AI-churn data point is a caution for Compose: AI generation lifts revenue per user but not retention. Don't lead with AI.

### Gaps
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Health & Fitness median prices, refund rates and monthly/annual retention curves could not be read (fetch blocked, report gated).
- No reliable data on lifetime-plan uptake or web-checkout share in H&F.

## 4. Major 2024–2026 trends

### Takeaway
Five forces define late 2026: **(1) AI coaches have become a standard feature** in every wearable and big app; **(2) consolidation**, in which the big platforms buy focused apps and valuations of 2021-era wellness winners collapse; **(3) GLP-1s are reshaping nutrition** away from calorie counting toward protein and logging by photo; **(4) phone and dopamine blockers** are a real, profitable niche (Opal, Brick, One Sec, the Gen Z "analog" movement); **(5) meditation is fatigued**: Headspace sold for ~10% of its peak valuation, Calm's revenue is falling, and both are turning to B2B or clinical care.

### Inferences
- **AI coaching no longer sets an app apart.** It is free or bundled with every wearable. Compose (AI meditations) should be framed as a feature that serves the override thesis (e.g. sessions for "the moment you don't want to start"), not as the headline.
- **Meditation as a standalone subscription is in decline**, but meditation as one part of a broader discipline routine is not tested by Calm's and Headspace's decline. Neuro-Nudge's Meditate should be sold as "training", not "calm".
- **The blocker niche shows demand for products about resisting impulses.** Opal and Brick sell the absence of a behaviour. Neuro-Nudge sells its opposite: acting when you don't want to. Same psychology (self-control), opposite direction. That is a clear pairing ("Opal stops you doing what you shouldn't; Neuro-Nudge gets you doing what you should").
- **GLP-1s weaken the calorie-counting incumbents' core.** That makes a non-calorie, food-group plate logger (Meals) better timed than it would have been in 2022.
- **Private small-group accountability is the direction social features are taking.** Pods (2–5 people) fit it. Many rivals are small, unfunded and marketed through self-published blogs, so no one owns the space yet.

### Gaps
- No shutdown list for 2025–2026 (beyond WeightWatchers' restructuring and Headspace's sale) was found.
- No TechCrunch/The Verge coverage could be fetched; launch dates for Apple Workout Buddy and Google Health Coach come from secondary blogs.
- Whether WeightWatchers emerged from Chapter 11 was not confirmed by these sources.

## 5. White space: what is underserved, especially for an effort/resistance metric

### Takeaway
Every incumbent measures **outcomes or physiology**: weight lifted (Hevy, Strong), pace and distance (Strava), calories (MFP), minutes meditated (Calm), recovery and strain from heart rate (Whoop, Oura), or streaks (habit apps). **None of the leaders found measures self-reported resistance to starting or persisting, or rewards hard days more.** That gap is real but unvalidated: no competitor or dataset proves demand for it.

### Cited Findings
- Whoop's "strain" and Oura's readiness scores are physiological (wearable-derived) and paid for through a hardware membership — [helsa.health](https://helsa.health/compare/best-ai-health-coach-apps); [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/digital-health-oura-vs-whoop)
- Lifting loggers (Hevy, Strong, Fitbod) compete on tracking volume and PRs at $24–96/yr — [SensAI comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-comparison)
- Habit trackers are a small, fragmented revenue pool (Streaks ~$0.6M, Habitify ~$0.36M); the outlier is Finch (~$30M), which wins on emotional framing (a self-care pet) rather than tracking — [Trends.vc](https://trends.vc/opportunities/companies/finch)
- Health & Fitness apps convert the first sale well but have the **lowest first-renewal retention of any category (30.3%, Adapty)**, and **~3–8% day-30 activity** — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/); [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/)
- Social accountability is moving toward small private groups, with no dominant player — [Habit Huddle](https://habithuddle.com/blog/fitness-accountability-app)
- Self-control products (Opal, Brick) reached eight-figure revenue or a viral following — [Speedinvest](https://www.speedinvest.com/knowledge/scaling-smart-how-opal-built-a-10m-arr-business-in-just-2-years); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/13/analog-gen-z-phone-addiction-bloom-brick-app-blockers-dumb-phones-social-media/)

### Inferences (the writer should present these as analysis, not findings)
- **A metric of the will across every domain.** Wearables score the body; loggers score output. No one scores the act of overcoming reluctance, across training, cold exposure, meditation, food and procrastinated tasks, in one number. This is Neuro-Nudge's most defensible claim, and it needs no hardware.
- **Low days are where incumbents lose people, and where Neuro-Nudge pays out most.** Outcome apps make a bad day look like failure (lower weight, slower pace, a broken streak). A metric where "harder to start" earns more credit flips the moment most apps lose users. That speaks directly to the category's day-30 and first-renewal problem, and it is the retention story for investors.
- **Bundling is validated, but only by acquisition.** Strava+Runna, MFP+Cal AI, Ladder+Nutrition and Sword+Headspace all show demand for one membership across domains. Neuro-Nudge reaches the bundle natively, tied together by one metric rather than stitched together through acquisitions.
- **The Huberman/Goggins buyer is already paying for the pieces.** Whoop or Oura ($70–360/yr), Hevy or Strong, a plunge-tracking spreadsheet, Opal. The opening is to be the app that sits alongside the wearable ("your Whoop measures strain; Neuro-Nudge measures will"), not to compete with it. Apple Health / wearable integration would strengthen that position.
- **Cold plunge / sauna logging** has no visible funded app competitor in these searches. It is a niche but culturally central to the target buyer.
- **Risks**: (1) self-reported 1–3 ratings may feel subjective or easy to game next to sensor data; (2) "effort scoring" can be copied as a feature (Whoop could add a "perceived effort to start" tag); (3) meditation and habit categories are shrinking or small, so don't lead with them; (4) with day-30 retention at 3–8%, Pods may be essential to retention rather than optional.

### Gaps
- No competitor found that explicitly scores resistance to starting or "activation energy" (a negative result from limited searching, not proof none exists).
- No survey data on how many consumers identify as "self-optimizers" or follow Huberman or Goggins. A market-sizing proxy, such as podcast audience or cold-plunge equipment sales, was not researched.
- No data on cold plunge / sauna app downloads or revenue.
