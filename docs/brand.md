*Worked out with the founder 2026-11-27, through one-question interviews built

**The founder edits the live version in the "Neuro-Nudge brand" doc**

Neuro-Nudge is an app **and** a brand: something people identify with and
wear, not only download. The money side is deliberately not decided here.

**Do hard shit (most of the time).** The pull of comfort is everywhere: your
phone, your feed and your couch are all built to make the easy choice
automatic. Override it. Not to suffer, but because small hard things are how
you get ready for the big ones. Neuro-Nudge counts every override, and the
hard ones count more.

*Founder's draft, still being considered:* "Neuro-Nudge is about doing the
shit you know you should be doing, and recognizing the effort is the reward."
(Fine as a belief; never present "the effort is the reward" as a scientific
claim. See Science guardrails.)

---

## The line

**Do hard shit (most of the time).**

The swear isn't the point; "most of the time" is. The first half is tough, the
second half is reasonable, and that turn is the whole brand. "Do hard things"
was rejected: it's a book title and a gym-wall cliché, and it has no second
half.

The App Store and ad platforms will need a clean version, not chosen yet.
Options so far: "Usually the hard way.", "The hard way, most of the time.",
"Do hard sh\*t most of the time." (the asterisk as part of the joke).

## The enemy: the pull

**You feel the pull of comfort every day.** Your phone, your feed, your fridge
and your couch are built to make the easy choice automatic. Most days, you go
along with it.

Introduce it once as "the pull of comfort", then just "the pull".

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where it comes from | Both. Products turned the difficulty down, and we let them. |
| How we treat it | Expose it, calmly. Show how the pull gets built in (autoplay, one-tap delivery, food built so you can't stop). Explain; never lecture. |
| What it should make people think | "I've been coasting." Honest recognition, no shame. |
| Shape | A force you feel in the moment, not a villain character. Give in to it often enough and you're coasting. |

Comfort is not the enemy. Comfort you never consciously chose is. It's not
reasonable to expect perfection or to swear off every modern luxury. What
matters is noticing the pull, and pushing outside your comfort zone on
purpose.

**Comfort Inc.**, a fictional company behind the pull, is parked. Revisit when
content starts (parody ads, "Comfort Inc. presents: autoplay").

**Rejected names**, so nobody re-proposes them: engineered easy (cold, too
intellectual), autopilot (no character), the lazy river, Easy Mode (close
second), Comfort Mode (chosen first, then dropped as bland), Big Comfort
(close, not quite). "The soft life" is off-limits: it's a positive self-care
movement for many people, especially Black women.

**Before merch:** "The pull" is a common phrase, so run a trademark check on
any exact merch line before it goes to print.

## The move: override

**Override the pull.** "Override" is already the app's word: every "how hard
was it to start?" rating is a logged override, so the brand and the product
say the same thing. An override is momentary and repeatable, which fits "most
of the time". The pull never goes away for good; you override it again today.

## Who our people are

**Neuro-Nudge people are capable.** Not perfect, not preachy, not in it for
the pain. They do hard shit most of the time, because small hard things are
practice for the big ones life will throw at them. They're openly proud of the
effort. They don't lecture anyone who gives in to the pull; they show what the
other way looks like, and explain if you ask.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Core trait | Capable. Not fragile; discomfort doesn't run them. |
| The payoff | Ready for life. The hard day you can't schedule (illness, loss, a big chance) is what the small overrides are practice for. |
| Never seen as | Preachy, perfect, masochistic. Try-hard was not ruled out: open pride in effort is fine. |
| Toward people who give in to the pull | Lead by example. Say nothing, be visibly capable, explain if asked. |
| What a shirt is for | Signalling to others. A person who never preaches lets the shirt do the talking. |
| Group name | None. Let it emerge: watch what pod members call themselves once Pods launches. |

The audience is anyone who worries modern life is making them soft. Use
"soft" with a wink ("modern life is making us soft, and honestly the couch is
very nice"), never as a sneer at other people.

## Voice

**Calm, self-aware, funny. Never alpha, never preachy.** Examples are drafts.

| Choice | Answer | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Humour | Self-aware. The joke is usually on us, including that we're an app on your phone telling you to use your phone less. | "You found us on Instagram. Good. Now close it." |
| Swearing | Mild, anywhere it sounds natural (shit, damn, hell). Sparingly; nothing stronger, and no slurs or gendered insults. | "Hard to start. Did it anyway. Damn." |
| Speaker | "We", the brand. (Merch lines can speak as the wearer: "I…") | "We built this because we kept skipping workouts too." |
| Science | Depends where. Ads and social: one plain idea. In-app "Why this works" and the website: go deeper, name studies. | "Your brain predicts the cost. You feel it as resistance." |
| Bad days | A self-aware joke, then move on. No guilt, no pep talk. | "The pull of comfort gets everyone now and then. Back at it?" |
| Wins | No applause. The rating already says it was hard, so we don't repeat it. Say nothing, state the plain fact, or undercut it with a joke. Never "you did the hard thing!" | "Nice work! Now refrain from posting about it on Instagram." |
| Rhythm | Mix. Short lines for headlines, buttons and moments; conversational sentences for explanations, emails and the origin story. | "Hard to start. Did it anyway." |

### Do and don't

| We sound like | We never sound like |
|---|---|
| A friend who lifts and still orders pizza | A drill sergeant |
| "The pull gets everyone now and then." | "No excuses." |
| "Woah… look at you making it look easy." | "LEGENDARY! You're UNSTOPPABLE!" |
| "Yes, we see the irony." | Holier-than-thou about screens or food |
| "Your brain predicts the cost." | "Boost your dopamine" or "grow your aMCC" |
| "Most of the time." | "Every single day." or "Never miss." |
| Showing how a feed keeps you | Telling people what they should eat |

### Words

- **Ours:** the pull, override, hard start, "a 3", most of the time, capable,
  "noted" (sparingly, or it becomes a tic).
- **Avoid:** alpha, beast, crush, grind, savage, warrior, "no excuses", "never
  miss", dopamine, "unlock your potential", "1% better", and "rep" (an existing app
  rule, see `docs/app-overview-user-facing.md`). "Practice" is fine (founder,
  2026-11-28).

### Science guardrails

The voice can be confident; the claims can't outrun the evidence.

- **Safe:** "your brain predicts the cost and you feel it as resistance";
  "starting is the part that becomes automatic"; "research links the aMCC to
  persistence"; "rewarding effort makes people choose harder things".
- **Not safe:** anything saying the app changes or grows your brain;
  "willpower is a muscle"; "it's always easier than you think"; dopamine or
  health claims for cold exposure; "you're getting stronger" as a measured
  fact. Say "see how hard your hard things feel to you, over time" instead.

## Where the app doesn't match yet

Found while writing this; nothing has changed yet. Tracked in
`docs/backlog.md` (Later).

- **Streak tiers are hype:** "On Fire", "Unstoppable", "Legendary"
  (`src/constants/willpower.ts`), shown by the celebration modal. Wins are
  meant to be understated.
- **The pull isn't named anywhere** in the app, site or onboarding. The
  landing page (`hosting/index.html`) uses "the 'I'll start tomorrow' voice"
  for the same idea.
- **Landing-page science lines** flagged in the research report
  ("strengthening the part of your brain…", "the effort region works
  hardest") break the guardrails above.
- **Mild swearing in the app** would likely raise the App Store age rating
  (Apple's "infrequent/mild profanity" setting). Check before shipping any.
